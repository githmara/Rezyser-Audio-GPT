Reżyser Audio GPT — Interactive Stories Module (18.22.0 – Release Edition)

The second main mode of the application alongside the Director — this time you become the player of a dynamically generated story instead of its author. Artificial intelligence narrates in the second person singular ("You walk through a misty forest, you smell the wet leaves..."), and you decide on the next move — by typing any action in free text or choosing one of the numbered options.

The module was designed from the start with blind players in mind — narration is read linearly by a screen reader (NVDA) or TTS engine, dynamic choices are presented as buttons with voice descriptions, each completed turn is signaled by a bell sound and automatic focus shift to the newly generated text.

You can open the panel with the "📖 Stories" button on the toolbar or the Ctrl+5 keyboard shortcut.

STEP 1: Three game modes — choose your playstyle

Just below the game name bar you will find the mode selector. The mode determines how the narrative engine conducts its dialogue with you:

FREE MODE — you type any action as free text ("I'll try to open the door", "I ask the guard for directions", "I escape down the back stairs"). The narrative engine may suggest 1–3 prompts, but these are hints only — not a mandatory path. The most open-ended mode, recommended for experienced players improvising their own story.

CHOICE MODE — each turn ends with 3–5 numbered options A–E presented as clickable buttons. Each option is a specific action you can take. You can also type your own action as free text — the engine will match it to the closest option semantically. The most intuitive mode for blind players: NVDA will read all options aloud, you Tab between them, and Enter confirms your selection.

LESSER EVIL MODE — like Choice Mode, but each of the 3–5 options is unfavorable — morally, physically, or strategically. There is no "neutral" option, no happy ending in that turn. A mode for fans of dark fiction in the spirit of "Tales of Consequence" or moral dramas.

The vial in Lesser Evil mode (from v15.2): after four turns of play, an additional, ZERO-numbered option "Uncork the vial" appears in the choice field — and remains there until the end of the game as a reusable choice (it does not disappear after use; you may reach for it multiple times). The vial is a "desperate rescue" option, but its effect is deliberately unpredictable and all the more dramatically weighty the rarer the result it yields: in approximately 60% of cases the contents of the vial are harmful (poisoning, wounding, blinding), in 30% it induces a perceptual disturbance or a spirit (hallucinations, altered speech, panic), and only in 10% of cases does it prove rarely beneficial (a momentary boost, a helpful spirit, a crucial piece of information). The distribution is enforced by Python (random.choices on the application side); the LLM receives a ready-made seed {kategoria, opis} and merely narrates it — the model has no way to "invent" a saving effect outside of the draw. As a result, the vial NEVER becomes a deus ex machina: even reaching for it in a moment of extreme hopelessness usually makes the situation worse. The choice of its narrative form (whether the vial definitively shatters or is tucked back into a belt) belongs to the narrative engine — sometimes after the first of the 60%-harmful doses you will never return.

The mode can be changed during the game, but only via the emergency button "Edit game mode…" next to the mode selector (from v15.2.3) — the selector itself is frozen on the established mode immediately after a game is created or loaded, analogously to the lock in the Director. The button opens a modal dialog with a warning; the typical use of this escape hatch is a situation in which the AI in Choice Mode or Lesser Evil Mode stubbornly generates further dilemmas instead of ending the story — switching to Free Mode then allows the player to describe the final scene themselves. If you want to start a second game in a different tone, simply type a new game name in the name field — the mode selector will unlock on its own, because the application recognizes that you are preparing a fresh project. Note: the vial is a feature exclusive to Lesser Evil Mode — when switching (through the escape hatch) within the same game to Choice Mode or Free Mode, its existence and the turn counter are preserved in the game state, but as long as you do not return to Lesser Evil Mode, the "Uncork the vial" option will not be displayed.

STEP 2: Quick Start — ready-made opening scenarios

Below the game name field you will find a second row labeled "Quick Start:" with a dropdown list. The default position is "(custom game)" — the engine will invent the world on its own based on your first action.

You can, however, choose one of five ready-made presets to immediately immerse yourself in a specific world:

POST-APOCALYPTIC SF — a century after an ecological catastrophe. The remnants of humanity in shelters beneath the Świętokrzyskie Mountains. You are a young courier of the Sztolnia Clan with your first serious assignment. Default mode: Choices.

DARK FANTASY — the borderlands of the Kingdom of Three Streams, the ninth year of a religious war. You are a junior Inquisitor on the trail of a village where someone is sheltering a possessed child. Default mode: Lesser Evil.

URBAN FANTASY DETECTIVE — Warsaw, 2008, an October evening. You are a private detective who can see ghosts. A female client wants to find her husband who went missing near the Royal Castle. Default mode: Choices.

COSMIC HORROR — the year 2247, a research station orbiting a neutron star. You wake from hibernation without a captain — and without any trace that the captain ever existed. Default mode: Free (a Lovecraftian atmosphere rewards improvisation).

ROMANTIC COMEDY DRAMA — Saska Kępa, April 2024, a small café facing closure. You are a barista-waitress whose owner tells you to "be nice" to a shy editor you have long wanted to approach yourself. Default mode: Free.

After selecting a preset, click "New Game" — the engine will inject a brief description of the world and the protagonist into itself, set the default game mode (you can override it with the mode selection field before clicking "New Game"). The first LLM turn will play out the opening scene.

STEP 3: First game from scratch — no preset

If you prefer complete freedom, leave "(custom game)" in the Quick Start field and get ready to type the first action manually.

  1. Enter the game name in the "Game Name" bar (e.g. "moja_przygoda_1") — the narration, state, and log files will be saved under this name.
  2. Select the mode (Freeform/Choices/Lesser Evil).
  3. Click "New Game". The application will create a new game and from this point on will automatically save its state. The file with the finished narration will be created after the first turn.
  4. In the "Your Action or Command" field at the bottom of the panel, type the first action, e.g. "I wake up in an unknown room". Click "Send". The narrative engine will open the world based on your action — a description of the room, sounds, characters, and the first decisions.
  5. Each turn will be saved automatically — you can close the application at any time and reload the game later using the "Load" button.

STEP 4: World Rules — custom rules that the engine respects throughout the game

From version 15.1, you will find a "Edit world rules…" button above the narration field. It opens a separate editing window with a multiline field where you can enter any rules — phonetic, mechanical, or dramaturgical — that the narrative engine will respect in every subsequent turn of this particular game. The rules are saved together with the game state, so loading the game preserves them unchanged.

Three typical applications of world rules:

PHONETIC RULES — control the pronunciation of character names by TTS (Vocalizer, ElevenLabs). Example: "Always pronounce the name Joanna with a hard, Polish [dʒ]; never soft [j]." The engine will insert this variant into the narration from the next turn, so that the name (e.g., Joanna) retains its intended sound in every subsequent scene of this game.

MECHANICAL RULES — describe the "laws of physics" of your world that the LLM must adhere to. Example: "In this world, magic only works at night; during the day, characters are ordinary people." The engine will not invent spells at noon, even if your turn's action demands it — instead, it will propose an alternative or guide you towards dusk.

DRAMATURGICAL RULES — define long-term tensions between characters. Example: "The antagonist Joana tries to soften the pronunciation of the protagonist's name through grammatical inflection." The engine weaves such subtle signals into Joana's dialogues, maintaining the conflict over many turns without your repeated instruction.

An empty rules field = no additional rules (default behavior, as in v15.0). You don't have to define rules right at the start — you can return to the window in any turn, add rules noticed during the game, and click "Save". The change takes effect from the next turn.

Note: world rules require an active game. If you click "Edit world rules…" without a loaded/started game, you will receive the message "No active game" — first click "New Game" or load a saved game.

STEP 5: Slash commands — quick actions without the API

In the action field you can, at any time, type a command starting with a slash /. Commands are local (they cost no API calls at all) and work regardless of the game mode. Both Polish and English variants are accepted:

/zapisz, /save — forces a manual save of the game state. Autosave runs after every turn, so this command is mainly there for peace of mind before closing the application.

/wczytaj, /load — opens a game-selection dialog with a list of available names (as of v15.2.3, this replaces the system file dialog — a direct list of all your saved games). NVDA reads the list naturally, so a blind player doesn't need to navigate the system file dialog. Once a game name is selected, the mode and the full narration are restored — you can continue from the turn where you left off. The "Load" button behaves identically.

The narrative engine of all Story modes uses one shared model (by default and recommendation, Anthropic Claude Sonnet 5; as of v18.4 an advanced user can point to their own OpenAI-compatible endpoint — see the main manual, STEP 2B). The stronger model adheres rigorously to the rules of the world — this matters especially in Lesser Evil mode, where every option must remain genuinely unfavorable.

/wizualizuj <opis>, /visualize <opis> — a multisensory description of the current scene. It does not change the game state and does not propose choices — the narrative engine uses all five senses (sight, hearing, smell, touch, taste) to render the scene with greater depth. The result appears in a separate dialog and is NOT appended to the narration. Example: "/wizualizuj jak wygląda strażnik" ("visualize what the guard looks like") or "/wizualizuj zapach kawiarni" ("visualize the smell of the café").

/pomoc, /help — displays a dialog box with a list of all available commands. The dialog is read-only and NVDA-friendly — the text field receives focus automatically, and the screen reader immediately reads out the entire list without requiring navigation.

/koniec, /quit — closes the application (after autosaving the turn in progress).

STEP 6: Your files — where to find the finished narration

Only one file matters from your perspective — the finished narration:

opowiesci/<nazwa>.txt — the complete narration ready to listen to as an audiobook, in the opowiesci/ folder next to the application. You can open it in Notepad, read it with a screen reader, or load it into the Audiobook Architect (Converter) and generate a Word document with chapters or a timeline for ElevenLabs.

The entire game state — characters, location, equipment, open plot threads, and the history of all turns so far — is managed automatically by the application. It saves the state after every turn in a hidden system folder that you don't need to (and shouldn't) touch. To load a saved game, use the "Load" button or the /wczytaj command — they will show you a list of all your games by name. This means you can close the application at any time and return to your game later, without worrying about saving anything manually.

STEP 7: Model memory indicator — why turns in old games don't require manual work from you

Below the mode selection field you will find the model memory indicator — a progress bar with a status description next to it. The indicator shows the percentage of the narrative engine's context window fill level (buffer counted up to 128,000 tokens).

Four fill levels:

🟢 clean (0%) — the game has just started, no saved turns.
🟢 OK (1-69%) — everything is normal, you can continue without restrictions.
⚠️ warning (70-89%) — the engine automatically generates a summary of previous turns in the background thread. A message will appear: "Auto-summarizing context in progress…" — do not send the next turn until the summary is complete. After success, the previous turns will be condensed into a single digest, the buffer will be freed, and you will receive a confirmation "Model memory recovered" with the number of condensed turns.
🚨 ALARM (90%+) — auto-summarization did not free the buffer (a rare case after an AI connection failure). Sending the next turn is blocked. Force the game to end via /koniec, then reload it — the engine will load the saved game state on its own without the history of the last turns, and the buffer will be fresh.

In practice, the auto-reduction mechanism is invisible — the player does not feel that anything is happening, because the background thread resolves the issue before it is noticed. The only important thing is that you do not send the next turn while auto-summarization is in progress (the "Send" button is grayed out at that time).

STEP 8: Cinematic Meta Warning — a dramatic interlude after turn 150

After 150 turns of a single game, the narrative engine will insert a "Cinematic Meta Warning" ONCE — a meta-narrative dramatic interlude. It will appear in a separate dialog with a bell sound, addressing you directly in the second person, summarizing your achievements and warning you about the weight of the decisions you are making.

The Cinematic Warning is NOT part of the audiobook narration — the application automatically cuts its content from the narration file (marked with ⚠️🚨⚠️ markers), so that during audiobook playback you will not hear a meta-commentary about your own game. You will see it only once, in a separate dialog window, where you can read it and calmly continue playing.

STEP 9: Accessibility (NVDA and screen readers) — designed from the ground up

The entire Stories module has been optimized for blind players. Here is what we specifically did:

LAST TURN FIELD — this is a separate read-only text field (several lines tall) ABOVE the full narration. After each turn, the field is replaced with a fresh fragment (the header "--- Turn N ---" + narration content). NVDA reads it immediately after focus is acquired (automatically after a successful turn), without navigating through hundreds of lines of the full history. After loading a saved game, the field shows a SUMMARY of the last saved turn (trimmed at a sentence boundary to approximately 1200 characters, so it never cuts off mid-word) with a prefix informing the user that the full narration is in the field below.

FULL NARRATION FIELD — this is a scrollable read-only text field that grows after each turn. NVDA reads the entire narration linearly; you can navigate within the text using arrow keys and copy excerpts with Ctrl+C. The field is never editable — you modify the story ONLY by submitting a new turn, using the new "Open narration file…" button (from v15.2.3, described below), or by directly editing the .txt file from File Explorer. This field is synchronized with the file opowiesci/<nazwa>.txt and can be treated as an audiobook preview.

THE "Open narration file…" BUTTON (from v15.2.3) — alongside the "New Game" / "Load" / "Save" buttons, a fourth button appears: "Open narration file…". It opens the file opowiesci/<nazwa>.txt in the system text editor (Notepad on Windows, TextEdit on macOS, the default GUI editor on Linux — analogously to how the Rule Manager opens YAML files or how the home panel opens golden_key.env). A typical use case is as an alternative to the "Edit game mode…" → Free gateway: instead of switching to a different mode to write the ending, you can simply open the .txt file and append the closing scene manually. WARNING ABOUT DESYNC RISK: before opening, the application displays a modal warning dialog, because the .txt file contains ONLY the narration for TTS, while the source of truth for the game state (characters, location, inventory, storylines) is a separate, hidden save file managed by the application itself. Editing the .txt does not update the state — the next LLM turn will receive the world as it was before your edit, so for example you could describe a character dying in the file while they remain alive in the game state and will appear in the next turn. Use this feature deliberately, ideally only to append a final scene after which you do not plan any further turns.

DYNAMIC CHOICE BUTTONS — in Choices and Lesser Evil modes, each turn creates new buttons in the choices area. Each has a tooltip read by NVDA ("Choose: continue into the forest"). Tab navigation between them works naturally. Pressing Enter on a focused button automatically sends that choice to the narrative engine.

HIDING EMPTY AREAS — in Free mode, the choices area is COMPLETELY hidden, not merely grayed out — Tab skips it and NVDA will not include it in the list of elements. This prevents player disorientation from landing on an empty placeholder. In modes 4 and 5, the area appears only after successful validation of the model's response (a non-empty choices array); a model hallucination (empty choices) triggers a fallback to free-text without cluttering the interface.

AUDIO SIGNALS — each completed turn is signaled by a system bell, followed by automatic focus transfer to the "Last Turn" field. NVDA will then read the fresh turn from the beginning, without requiring navigation through the entire history so far.

COLOR-CODED MEMORY STATUSES — the model memory indicator changes font color (green / orange / red) according to the fill level. For sighted users this is an additional visual signal; NVDA reads the status text regardless, so blind users lose no information.

KEYBOARD ACCELERATORS — all buttons have an & character before one of the letters in their label, which activates an Alt+letter accelerator. This works identically to other modules in the application.

Most Common Problems

Problem: After clicking "New Game", the message "No API key" appears.
Cause and solution: The golden_key.env file in the application's main folder does not have a valid Anthropic key (this key powers the Stories mode). Go back to the start screen (Home button), click "Generate the golden_key.env file and open the text editor" and follow the instructions (STEP 2 of the main manual).

Problem: I'm trying to send the next turn, but the "Send" button is grayed out.
Cause and solution: Context auto-summarization or Cinematic Warning generation is running in a background thread. The memory status will show "Context auto-summarization in progress…". Wait a few seconds — the button will unlock automatically upon success.

Problem: I clicked a choice, but instead of being sent, its content (without the letter A-E) ended up in the action field.
Cause and solution: This is the same situation as the problem above — the "Send" button was momentarily grayed out at that moment (auto-summarization or Cinematic Warning was running in the background). Normally, clicking a choice is a ready move and it goes straight to the engine; however, when sending is temporarily blocked, the application doesn't lose your choice — it instead types its content into the action field and places the cursor there. You then have two options. First: wait a few seconds for the button to unlock and send the choice as-is — treating it as a ready draft (this is the default behavior from version 15.1 and later). Second: completely replace the content in the field with your own — this is a particularly good moment right after a Cinematic Meta Warning, when instead of clicking the next dilemma you prefer to type a clear command to wrap up the story and open threads, if you deem it appropriate. After editing, click "Send" (or press Enter).

Problem: The message "Model memory overloaded" is blocking the game.
Cause and solution: Auto-summarization did not free up the buffer (e.g., a temporary request limit on the AI provider's side). End the game with the /koniec command or close the application, then reload it — the engine will load the saved game state on its own, without the history of the last turns.

Problem: In Choices mode, the engine did not generate buttons, even though I expected them.
Cause and solution: Model hallucination — despite mode 4, the LLM returned an empty choices array. The application intentionally hides the choices area in this case (instead of showing an empty placeholder) and allows you to continue with free text. The next turn will most likely generate choices correctly.

Problem: I can't see any save files for my game.
Cause and solution: This is by design — the application keeps the game state in a hidden system folder and manages it itself, so you don't need to look for it or save anything manually. The only file you actually need is the finished narrative in the opowiesci/ folder next to the application — you'll find it there. You can load saved games using the "Load" button or the /wczytaj command.
