Audio Director GPT — Interactive Stories Module (15.5.1 – Release Edition)

The second main mode of the application alongside the Director — this time you become the player of a dynamically generated story instead of its author. Artificial intelligence narrates in the second person singular ("You walk through a misty forest, you smell the wet leaves..."), and you decide on the next move — by typing any action in free text or choosing one of the numbered options.

The module was designed from the start with blind players in mind — narration is read linearly by a screen reader (NVDA) or TTS engine, dynamic choices are presented as buttons with voice descriptions, each completed turn is signaled by a bell sound and automatic focus shift to the newly generated text.

You can open the panel with the "📖  Stories" button on the toolbar or the Ctrl+5 keyboard shortcut.


STEP 1: Three Game Modes — Choose Your Play Style

Just below the game title bar, you'll find a mode selection box (RadioBox). The mode determines how the narrative engine engages in dialogue with you:

FREE MODE — you enter any action in free text ("I'll try to open the door," "I ask the guard for directions," "I escape through the back stairs"). The narrative engine may suggest 1-3 options, but these are just hints — not mandatory paths. The most free-form mode, recommended for experienced players improvising their own story.

CHOICE MODE — each turn ends with 3-5 numbered options A-E in the form of clickable buttons. Each choice is a specific action you can take. You can also enter your own action in free text — the engine will match it to the closest semantic choice. The most intuitive mode for visually impaired players: NVDA will read all options to you, you can tab between them, and press Enter to confirm.

LESSER EVIL MODE — similar to Choice Mode, but each of the 3-5 options is morally, physically, or strategically disadvantageous. No "neutral" option, no happy ending in this turn. A mode for fans of dark fiction in the style of "Tales of Consequence" or moral dramas.

Vial in Lesser Evil Mode (from v15.2): after four turns of play, an additional, ZERO-numbered option "Uncork the vial" appears in the choice field — and remains there for the rest of the game as a reusable choice (it doesn't disappear after use, you can reach for it repeatedly). The vial is a "desperate rescue" option, but its effect is intentionally unpredictable and the more dramatically severe, the rarer the beneficial result: in about 60% of cases, the vial's contents are harmful (poisoning, injuring, blinding), in 30% it causes perception or spirit disturbance (hallucinations, altered speech, panic), and only in 10% it proves rarely beneficial (temporary enhancement, helpful spirit, key information). The distribution is enforced by Python (`random.choices` on the application side), the LLM receives a ready seed `{category, description}` and merely narrationalizes it — the model cannot "invent" a salvific outcome beyond the draw. Thanks to this, the vial NEVER becomes a deus ex machina: even reaching for it in a moment of extreme despair usually worsens the situation. The choice of its narrative form (whether the vial definitively shatters or is put back in the belt) belongs to the narrative engine — sometimes after the first of the 60%-harmful doses, you never return.

The mode can be changed during the game, but only through the emergency "Edit game mode…" button next to the RadioBox (from v15.2.3) — the RadioBox itself is frozen on the locked-in mode immediately after creating or loading a game, analogous to the Director's protection. The button opens a modal dialog with a warning; the typical use of this loophole is a situation in which the AI in Choices or Lesser Evil Mode stubbornly generates further dilemmas instead of ending the story — switching to Free Mode then allows the player to describe the final scene themselves. If you want to start a second game in a different tonality, simply type a new game name in the name field — the RadioBox will unlock by itself, because the application recognizes you are preparing a fresh project. Note: the vial is a feature exclusive to Lesser Evil Mode — when switching (through the loophole) in the same game to Choices or Free Mode, its existence and turn counter remain preserved in the game state, but until you return to Lesser Evil, the "Uncork the vial" option will not be displayed.


STEP 2: Quick Start — ready-made opening scenarios

Below the game name field, you'll find a second row labeled "Quick Start:" with a dropdown selection (wx.Choice). The default position is "(custom game)" — the engine will invent the world based on your first action.

However, you can choose one of five ready-made presets to immediately immerse yourself in a specific world:

POST-APOCALYPTIC SF — a century after an ecological disaster. The remnants of humanity in shelters under the Świętokrzyskie Mountains. You are a young courier from the Mine Clan with your first serious assignment. Default mode: Choices.

DARK FANTASY — the border of the Kingdom of Three Streams, the ninth year of a religious war. You are a junior Inquisitor on the trail of a village where someone is protecting a possessed child. Default mode: Lesser Evil.

URBAN FANTASY DETECTIVE — Warsaw 2008, an October evening. You are a private detective who sees ghosts. A client wants to find her husband who disappeared under the Royal Castle. Default mode: Choices.

COSMIC HORROR — the year 2247, a research station orbiting a neutron star. You wake up from hibernation without the captain — and without any traces that the captain ever existed. Default mode: Free (Lovecraftian atmosphere favors improvisation).

ROMANTIC COMEDY OF MANNERS — Saska Kępa, April 2024, a small café faces closure. You are a barista-waitress whose owner tells you to "be nice" to a shy editor you've long wanted to approach yourself. Default mode: Free.

After selecting a preset, click "New Game" — the engine will inject a short description of the world and the hero, set the default game mode (you can override it with the RadioBox before clicking "New Game"). The first LLM turn will play out the opening scene.


STEP 3: First game from scratch — without a preset

If you prefer complete freedom, leave "(custom game)" in the Quick Start field and prepare to enter the first action manually.

1. Enter the name of the game in the "Game Name" bar (e.g., "my_adventure_1") — under this name, the narrative, state, and log files will be saved.
2. Choose the mode (Free/Choices/Lesser Evil).
3. Click "New Game". The application will create 3 files: game state (.game.json), an empty World Book (.md), and mode metadata (.mode). The narrative file (.txt) will be created after the first turn.
4. In the "Your Action or Command" field at the bottom of the panel, enter the first action, e.g., "I wake up in an unknown room." Click "Send". The narrative engine will open the world based on your action — room description, sounds, characters, initial decisions.
5. Each turn will be automatically saved — you can close the application at any time and load the game later with the "Load" button.


STEP 4: World Rules — custom rules that the engine respects throughout the game

From version 15.1, you will find a "Edit world rules…" button above the narration field. It opens a separate editing window with a multiline field where you can enter any rules — phonetic, mechanical, or dramaturgical — that the narrative engine will respect in every subsequent turn of this particular game. The rules are saved together with the game state (.game.json), so loading the game preserves them unchanged.

Three typical applications of world rules:

PHONETIC RULES — control the pronunciation of character names by TTS (Vocalizer, ElevenLabs). Example: "Always pronounce the name Joanna with a hard, Polish [dʒ]; never soft [j]." The engine will insert this variant into the narration from the next turn, and the character Joanna in the World Book will retain the intended sound. This is also a natural bridge to the Director's accents — if you load the Book into the Director's panel after the game (STEP 9 below), the phonetic rule will "carry over" with the game as a character trait.

MECHANICAL RULES — describe the "laws of physics" of your world that the LLM must adhere to. Example: "In this world, magic only works at night; during the day, characters are ordinary people." The engine will not invent spells at noon, even if your turn's action demands it — instead, it will propose an alternative or guide you towards dusk.

DRAMATURGICAL RULES — define long-term tensions between characters. Example: "The antagonist Joana tries to soften the pronunciation of the protagonist's name through grammatical inflection." The engine weaves such subtle signals into Joana's dialogues, maintaining the conflict over many turns without your repeated instruction.

An empty rules field = no additional rules (default behavior, as in v15.0). You don't have to define rules right at the start — you can return to the window in any turn, add rules noticed during the game, and click "Save". The change takes effect from the next turn.

Note: world rules require an active game. If you click "Edit world rules…" without a loaded/started game, you will receive the message "No active game" — first click "New Game" or load a saved game.


STEP 5: Slash Commands — Quick Actions Without API

In the action field, you can enter a command starting with a slash `/` at any time. Commands are local (do not cost calls to OpenAI) and work independently of the game mode. Both Polish and English variants are accepted:

/zapisz, /save — forces a manual save of the game state. Autosave occurs after each turn, so this command is mainly for assurance before closing the application.

/wczytaj, /load — opens a game selection dialog with a list of available names (from v15.2.3 instead of the system file dialog — a direct list of saved games from the runtime/opowiesci/ folder, detected by the presence of a .game.json file). NVDA reads the list naturally, the blind player doesn't have to navigate the system file dialog. After selection, the game name, mode, and full narration are restored — you can continue from the turn where you left off. The "Load" button has identical behavior.

The narrative engine automatically selects the AI model depending on the mode: for the Choices and Lesser Evil modes, it uses the more expensive gpt-4o (strictly adheres to the world's rules), for the Free mode — the faster and cheaper gpt-4o-mini (the player controls the plot, so dramatic direction is not required).

/wizualizuj <description>, /visualize <description> — a multisensory description of the current scene. It does not change the game state, does not propose choices — the narrative engine uses all five senses (sight, hearing, smell, touch, taste) to show the scene with greater depth. The result appears in a separate dialog, it is NOT added to the narration or saved in the audiobook file. Example: "/wizualizuj how the guard looks" or "/wizualizuj the smell of the café".

/scena <description>, /scene <description> — current event ELSEWHERE, told in the third person. The camera detaches from your character and shows what is happening in parallel with another character or in another part of the world. Example: "/scene Anna at the marketplace" or "/scene the guards at the gate". The engine weaves the scene into the narration, marks it with the "Off-screen scene:" prefix, and automatically returns to your character on the next turn. Choices do not appear — the player does not make decisions inside someone else's scene. Full description of the mechanism in STEP 5B.

/flashback <description>, /retrospekcja <description> — retrospection in the third person, a step back in time. Example: "/flashback the day father died" or "/flashback the protagonists' first meeting". Opens the narration with a temporal formula ("Three years earlier…"), tags it with the "Flashback:" prefix, and returns to the current turn. Identical to /scene — passive, no choices. Full description in STEP 5B.

/odswiez, /refresh — refreshes the internal memory from the narration file after you have edited it by hand (since v15.5). When you open opowiesci/<name>.txt in an editor and, for example, cut off a cinematic cut that the engine wrongly closed, this command (or the "Refresh from disk" button) synchronizes the fresh ending with the engine's context — without a hard reset and a full reload of the game. If a summary of earlier turns already exists, it stays untouched, so the next turn and the next summary are not built on stale data. NOTE: the game state (characters, location, inventory, threads) from the .game.json file is NOT updated — if your edit changed facts of the world, the next turn may drift out of sync with them.

/pomoc, /help — displays a dialog with the list of all available commands. The dialog is read-only and NVDA-friendly — the text field receives focus automatically, the screen reader immediately reads the whole list without manual navigation.

/koniec, /quit — closes the application (after autosaving the current turn).


STEP 5B: The second camera — off-screen scenes and flashbacks (since v15.4)

By default you play in first person — the narration says "you walk", "you feel", "you see". Since version 15.4 you can direct a scene from a distance, told in the third person, not tied directly to your character. Two commands do this: /scena for parallel events (somewhere else in the world), /flashback for retrospection (in the past). After the cut the engine returns to your character on the next turn.

Why this exists. A classic narrative game limits all events to one character's point of view. The second camera lets you build drama from a broader perspective — show the antagonist fleeing the city, Anna reading your letter thirty kilometers away, the same tavern where your father died ten years ago. Without this mechanism the player learns about such things only from NPC accounts; now they can witness them live.

What the narration looks like. The engine opens the scene with a formula signaling the shift of perspective — for /scena: "Meanwhile…", "Five kilometers away…"; for /flashback: "Three years earlier…", "When she was seventeen…". In the narration panel and in the "Last turn" field the text is additionally preceded by an A11y prefix: "Off-screen scene:" or "Flashback:". This prefix is also written to the narrative .txt file — when you open the audiobook in another TTS reader you will still know the camera has shifted.

What to expect from choices. In third-person scenes the choice buttons field is ALWAYS empty — the player does not make decisions for the antagonist or for their younger self. The next turn returns to second person and proposes choices according to the active mode (Free, Choices, Lesser Evil).

Engine auto-cuts. Independently of your commands, the narrative engine may decide on its own that a moment deserves a cinematic cut — it then inserts a short off-screen scene with the "Meanwhile elsewhere:" prefix. These organic interludes are limited: at most one auto-cut per arc stage (exposition, rising action, climax, resolution), so at most four per story. The limit applies only to engine-initiated cuts; your /scena and /flashback are unlimited. The counter persists in the .game.json save file — when you return to the game after three days, the per-stage limit will be honored.

Practical tip. The /scena command works great for building tension just before the climax ("/scena guards approaching from the gate" — you know they are coming, your character does not yet). /flashback works best as a way to explain an NPC's motivation ("/flashback Marek as a child, the day of the fire"). Do not overuse — narration thick with cut-scenes loses the first-person rhythm that is the core of this mode.


STEP 6: Game Files — where to find your data

Each game produces five files, each in a different folder, with various roles:

opowiesci/<name>.txt — the full narrative ready for listening as an audiobook. It can be opened in Notepad or loaded into the Audiobook Architect (Converter) to generate a Word file with chapters or timing for ElevenLabs.

opowiesci/<name>.md — the World Book of characters in the format [Name: traits], regenerated after each turn. The format is compatible with the Director's parser! You can load this file into the Director's panel (File → Load) and receive native character accents in classic TTS narration — a bridge between the two main modules of the application.

runtime/opowiesci/<name>.game.json — the full game state: name, mode, active characters, location, inventory, open threads, the last 6 turns in a summarized form. This is the source of truth for loading the game — without this file, you cannot continue.

runtime/opowiesci/<name>.story.jsonl — the raw log of each turn (player request + model response in JSON). This file is not needed for the game — it is used for debugging or later analysis (e.g., statistics on how many turns the adventure lasted, what threads the LLM introduced).

runtime/skrypty/<name>.mode — metadata of the mode number (3=Free, 4=Choices, 5=Lesser Evil). This file is shared with the Director, but the numbering does not conflict (the Director uses 0-2).


STEP 7: Model Memory Indicator — Why Turns in Old Games Don't Require Manual Work from You

Below the mode RadioBox, you'll find the model memory indicator — a progress bar (wx.Gauge) with a status description next to it. The indicator shows the percentage of the narrative engine's context window filled (gpt-4o-mini has a buffer of 128,000 tokens).

Four levels of fill:

🟢 clean (0%) — the game has just started, no turns recorded.
🟢 OK (1-69%) — everything is normal, you can continue without restrictions.
⚠️ warning (70-89%) — the engine automatically generates a summary of previous turns in the background thread. A message "Auto-summarizing context in progress..." will appear — do not send another turn until the summarization is complete. Upon success, previous turns will be condensed into a single summary, the buffer will be freed, and you will receive confirmation "Model memory recovered" with the number of condensed turns.
🚨 ALARM (90%+) — auto-summarization did not free the buffer (a rare case after an API failure). Sending another turn is blocked. Force the game to end with /end, then reload it — the engine will load the state from game.json without the history of recent turns, and the buffer will be fresh.

In practice, the auto-reduction mechanism is invisible — the player doesn't feel that anything is happening because the background thread solves the problem before it becomes noticeable. It's only important not to send another turn during auto-summarization (the "Send" button is grayed out at that time).


STEP 8: Cinematic Meta Warning — dramatic interlude after the 150th turn

After 150 turns in a single game, the narrative engine will ONCE insert a "Cinematic Meta Warning" — a meta-narrative dramatic interlude. It will appear in a separate dialogue with a bell sound, directly addressing you in the second person, summarizing achievements, and warning about the significance of the decisions being made.

The Cinematic Warning is NOT part of the audiobook narration — the .txt filtering engine automatically removes its content (marked with ⚠️🚨⚠️) so that you don't hear meta-commentary about your own game during the audiobook playback. The content is only logged in the .story.jsonl file (for reflection or as a keepsake) and in the GUI dialogue, where you can read it once and continue.


STEP 9: Bridge to the Director — Utilize the World Book Created in Stories

The file opowiesci/<name>.md generated during gameplay contains the World Book with characters that appeared in the narrative, along with their traits, accents, and character. The format is exactly what the Director's parser requires — [Name: traits with accent description].

What you can do with it:

1. After finishing the game, open the "🎬  Director" panel (Ctrl+1).
2. Load a project with the same name — the Director will load the .txt file as a story and the .md file as the World Book.
3. You will now receive the classic Director's narrative (long, structured scenes with acts and chapters) with the native accents of characters you liked in Stories. The Carmel Guardian from the shelter speaking in short sentences, the inquisitor Rosanna with a melodic eastern accent — all traits preserved.

This is a one-way bridge: Stories produce material, the Director formats it. The reverse is not possible — the Director does not load games from Stories as an interactive state.


STEP 10: Accessibility (NVDA and screen readers) — designed from the start

The entire Stories module has been optimized for visually impaired users. Here's what we specifically did:

LAST TURN FIELD — this is a separate wx.TextCtrl readonly multiline (~140 px height) ABOVE the full narration. After each turn, the field is replaced with a fresh fragment (header "--- Turn N ---" + narration content). NVDA reads it immediately upon focus (auto-focus after a successful turn), without navigating through hundreds of lines of the full story. After loading a saved game, the field shows a SUMMARY of the last saved turn (max 400 characters from the `last_turns` buffer) with a prefix informing that the full narration is in the field below.

FULL NARRATION FIELD — this is a wx.TextCtrl readonly multiline that grows with each turn. NVDA reads the entire narration linearly, you can navigate with arrows within the text, copy fragments with Ctrl+C. The field is never editable — you modify the story ONLY through a new turn, through the new "Open narration file…" button (since v15.2.3, described below), or by direct editing of the .txt file from the file explorer. This field is synchronized with the `opowiesci/<name>.txt` file, you can treat it as a preview of the audiobook.

"Open narration file…" BUTTON (since v15.2.3) — next to the "New Game"/"Load"/"Save" buttons, a fourth one appears: "Open narration file…". It opens the `opowiesci/<name>.txt` file in the system text editor (Notepad on Windows, TextEdit on macOS, default GUI editor on Linux — analogous to how the Rule Manager opens YAML files or how the home panel opens `golden_key.env`). Typical use-case is an alternative to the "Edit game mode…" loophole → Free: instead of jumping to a different mode to write the finale, you can simply open the .txt file and manually add the closing scene. WARNING ABOUT DIVERGENCE RISK: before opening, the application shows a modal warning dialog, because .txt is ONLY the narration for TTS, while the source of truth of the game state (characters, location, inventory, threads) is a separate `.game.json` file. Editing the .txt does not update the state — the next LLM turn will receive the world from before your edit, so e.g. you may describe in the file that a character died, but in the game state they will still be alive and will appear in the next turn. Use deliberately, ideally only to add the closing scene after which you don't plan further turns anyway.

DYNAMIC CHOICE BUTTONS — in Choices and Lesser Evil modes, each turn creates new wx.Buttons in the choice area. Each has a tooltip read by NVDA ("Choose: go further into the forest"). Tab between them works naturally. Enter on the selected button auto-sends that choice to the narrative engine.

HIDING EMPTY AREAS — in Free mode, the choice area is COMPLETELY hidden (wx.Hide), not just grayed out — Tab skips it, NVDA won't include it in the list of elements. This prevents player confusion from falling into an empty placeholder. In modes 4 and 5, the area appears only after successful model response validation (non-empty choice array); model hallucination (empty choices) results in a fallback to free-text without cluttering the interface.

AUDIO SIGNALS — each completed turn is signaled by wx.Bell (system bell), followed by automatic focus transfer to the narration field. NVDA will then read the new narration without the need for manual navigation.

COLORED MEMORY STATUS — the model memory indicator changes font color (green/orange/red) according to the fill level. For sighted users, this is an additional visual cue; NVDA still reads the status text, so visually impaired users don't miss out on information.

KEYBOARD ACCELERATORS — all buttons have a `&` sign before one of the label letters, which activates the Alt+letter accelerator. It works identically to other application modules.


Most Common Problems

Problem: After clicking "New Game", the message "No API Key" appears.
Cause and Solution: The golden_key.env file in the main application folder does not have a valid OpenAI key. Return to the start screen (Home button), click "Generate the golden_key.env file and open the text editor" and follow the instructions (STEP 2 of the main manual).

Problem: I am trying to send another turn, but the "Send" button is grayed out.
Cause and Solution: Context auto-summarization or Cinematic Warning generation is in progress in the background thread. The memory status will show "Context auto-summarization in progress...". Wait a few seconds — the button will automatically unlock upon success.

Problem: The message "Model memory overloaded" blocks the game.
Cause and Solution: Auto-summarization did not release the buffer (e.g., OpenAI rate limit). End the game with the /end command or close the application, then reload it — the engine will load only the state from game.json without the history of recent turns.

Problem: In Choice mode, the engine did not generate buttons, even though I expected them.
Cause and Solution: Model hallucination — despite mode 4, the LLM returned an empty choice array. The application consciously hides the choice area (instead of showing an empty placeholder) and allows continuation with free-text. The next turn will likely generate choices correctly.

Problem: The .game.json and .story.jsonl files are not visible on the desktop.
Cause and Solution: This is intentional — runtime/ is a system folder for regular users, invisible from the file explorer. Narrative files (.txt, .md) go to scripts/ — you will find them there.
